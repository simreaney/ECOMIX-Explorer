Using the ECOMIX Explorer

This dashboard lets you explore catchment data, real measured records, modelled water body concentrations, and model projections for the ECOMIX study area. Use the tabs at the top of the page to move between the different tools described below.

Map

Click a subbasin on the map to select it. Details for the selected subbasin appear in the panel on the right, which can be dragged to a different position on the screen if it is in your way. The Map also has toggleable layers for measured chemical-monitoring sites and modelled water bodies - click a marker or water body polygon's popup link to open its detailed record on the Site Details tab.

Site Details

Displays a chemical occurrence/concentration grid and a time series plot for a selected measured site or modelled water body. Use the "Measured" / "Modelled" toggle at the top of the sidebar list to switch between the two, and the sidebar dropdown to choose which chemical parameter is shown in the time series.

Chemicals in the measured and modelled datasets

The measured sites test for 48 organic micropollutants, 2 metals and 5 physicochemical parameters; the modelled water bodies cover the same 26 organic micropollutants/metals as monthly modelled concentrations. Brief notes on what each substance is, where it typically comes from, and its likely ecological impact are given below, grouped by chemical type.

Chemical(s) What it is & where it comes from Likely ecological impact
Human antibiotics
Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin		 Macrolide antibiotics prescribed for bacterial infections; excreted largely unmetabolised and discharged via wastewater treatment works (WwTWs). Persistent in water; can suppress sensitive algae and bacteria and promote antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquatic microbial communities.
Veterinary antibiotics
Lincomycin, Tylosin		 Antibiotics used mainly in livestock farming (e.g. pigs, poultry); reach rivers via manure spreading, farm runoff and slurry storage. Same AMR and microbial-community concerns as human antibiotics; often co-occur with nutrient runoff from the same sources.
Anticonvulsants / antidepressants
Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine, Desvenlafaxine		 Prescription drugs for epilepsy (carbamazepine, lamotrigine) and depression (desvenlafaxine, the active metabolite of venlafaxine); poorly removed by conventional sewage treatment. Carbamazepine is so persistent it is widely used as a chemical marker of treated wastewater. Chronic low-level exposure has been linked to altered fish behaviour, including feeding activity and predator avoidance.
NSAIDs
Diclofenac, Ibuprofen		 Common over-the-counter and prescription painkillers, excreted after use and discharged via WwTWs. Diclofenac is well known for causing catastrophic vulture population declines in South Asia via livestock carcasses; in fish it is linked to kidney and gill damage at elevated concentrations.
Other human pharmaceuticals
Metformin, Telmisartan, Cetirizine, Fluconazole, Miconazole		 Widely used medicines for diabetes (metformin), blood pressure (telmisartan), allergies (cetirizine) and fungal infections (fluconazole, miconazole); enter rivers via treated and untreated sewage. Generally low acute toxicity individually, but metformin and telmisartan are among the highest-concentration pharmaceuticals found in UK rivers; antifungals can affect non-target fungi and algae and contribute to antifungal resistance.
Caffeine Stimulant from tea, coffee and soft drinks; excreted by humans and discharged via WwTWs and combined sewer overflows. Low ecotoxicity, but is a well-established chemical tracer for detecting sewage contamination in rivers.
UV filters
Benzophenone-3, Homosalate, Octocrylene		 Sunscreen and cosmetic ingredients, washed off skin during bathing/swimming and discharged via WwTWs. Suspected endocrine disruptors; associated with coral bleaching in marine systems and oestrogenic effects in fish.
Preservatives / biocides
Methylchloroisothiazolinone, Methylisothiazolinone, Octenidine Dihydrochloride		 Antimicrobial preservatives (MCI/MIT) used in cosmetics and household cleaning products, and an antiseptic (octenidine) used in medical and personal-care products; discharged via WwTWs. Toxic to aquatic invertebrates and algae even at low concentrations; MCI/MIT are also common skin sensitisers.
Veterinary parasiticides
Flubendazole, Fluralaner		 Flubendazole is a livestock and companion-animal dewormer; fluralaner is a flea/tick treatment for pets. Enter rivers via manure, and via WwTWs from bathing or washing treated pets. Fluralaner is an insecticide (isoxazoline class) and is highly toxic to aquatic invertebrates such as shrimp and insect larvae.
Herbicides
Bentazone, Metazachlor, Dimethenamid, Flufenacet, Triallate		 Weed-control products used mainly on cereal and oilseed rape crops; reach rivers via agricultural field runoff and land drainage. Toxic to non-target aquatic plants and algae; several are mobile and persistent enough to reach groundwater and drinking-water sources.
Fungicides
Azoxystrobin, Boscalid, Difenoconazole, Dimoxystrobin, Metconazole, Pyraclostrobin, Tebuconazole, Trifloxystrobin		 Applied to arable and horticultural crops to control fungal disease; reach rivers via agricultural runoff. Toxic to aquatic fungi and algae, disrupting leaf-litter breakdown and nutrient cycling; several (e.g. the strobilurins azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, trifloxystrobin) are also highly toxic to fish and invertebrates.
Insecticides
Fipronil, Imidacloprid, Permethrin, Tefluthrin		 Used in agriculture (seed treatments, crop sprays) and, for fipronil and imidacloprid, in veterinary flea treatments; enter rivers via field runoff and washing of treated pets. Neonicotinoids (imidacloprid) and phenylpyrazoles (fipronil) are extremely toxic to aquatic insects even at very low (nanogram-per-litre) concentrations, and have been linked to declines in invertebrate abundance in UK rivers.
6PPD-Q A transformation product of 6PPD, an antioxidant added to vehicle tyres; formed when tyre-wear particles react with ozone and washed off roads in surface runoff. Acutely lethal to salmonid fish (e.g. coho salmon, trout) at very low concentrations - one of the most acutely toxic substances known for this group and a likely contributor to unexplained urban-runoff fish kills.
PFAS
PFOA, PFOS		 "Forever chemicals" historically used in firefighting foams, waterproofing and non-stick coatings; extremely persistent and reach rivers via industrial and urban discharge and legacy contamination. Do not break down in the environment; bioaccumulate up the food chain and are linked to liver toxicity, immune suppression and reproductive effects in wildlife.
Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)
Anthracene, Benzo[a]pyrene, Chrysene, Fluoranthene, Methyl Anthracene, Methyl Phenanthrene, Phenanthrene, Pyrene		 Formed by incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and organic matter (vehicle exhaust, tyre and road wear, coal and wood burning); washed into rivers in urban surface runoff. Several, including benzo[a]pyrene, are carcinogenic and mutagenic; toxic to fish and invertebrates and tend to accumulate in river sediment.
Metals
Zinc (Zn), Copper (Cu)		 Sources include tyre and brake wear, roofing and guttering, industrial discharge, and legacy mining in the Pennine headwaters. Essential micronutrients at trace levels but toxic to fish and invertebrates at elevated concentrations, particularly damaging to gill function.
pH A measure of acidity/alkalinity, influenced by catchment geology, upland peat/moorland drainage and wastewater discharge. Values outside the normal range stress aquatic life directly and can increase the toxicity of ammonia and dissolved metals.
Dissolved organic carbon (DOC) Naturally derived from peat and soil runoff in upland catchments, supplemented by wastewater inputs. Affects light penetration and the binding/bioavailability of metals and organic chemicals; high DOC can also increase disinfection by-product formation in drinking-water treatment.
Nutrients
Nitrate, Ammonium, Phosphate		 Derived from agricultural fertiliser and livestock manure runoff, and from sewage effluent. Drive eutrophication - excessive algal growth, oxygen depletion and loss of biodiversity; ammonium is also directly toxic to fish at elevated concentrations.

Data Explorer

Select one or more scenarios in the sidebar to compare climate, observed and projected data for the currently selected subcatchment. The summary boxes at the top show key information about the selection, and the plots below can be expanded to full screen using the icon in the top-right corner of each card. The projections card has separate tabs for yearly, monthly and cumulative-frequency views. Use the "Subbasin" / "Water body" toggle at the top of the sidebar to switch to a modelled water body's chemical concentration series instead - climate and observed hydrology have no water-body equivalent, so those cards are unavailable in that mode.

Spatial Datasets

Choose a model output variable from the sidebar to view it mapped across the catchment - including, for chemical variables, either the subbasin-level daily dataset or the water body-level modelled monthly dataset.

Data Downloader

Select a variable, data type, spatial layer and file format in the sidebar, then use the Download Data button to save the corresponding table. The table is also previewed on this tab before downloading.

Food Web Dynamics

Embeds the interactive food-web dynamic model. This requires the associated model server to be running locally.
